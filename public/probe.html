Can a picture's address be read?

Paste picture addresses below and this reports, for each one, whether the browser can DISPLAY it and whether it can READ ITS BYTES. Those are different questions and only the second one decides whether the app could ever store a picture from a link.

Running this contacts those sites from your machine — that is the whole point of it, and it is the same request the app would have to make. Nothing is stored, nothing is sent anywhere else, and closing the page ends it.

The policy this page is running under

Read from this page's own response, not from what it expects to have been sent. If this is the app's policy rather than the probe's, every result below would be a refusal by us and would say nothing about the other site.

How to read a result

  • Displays: yes — Bytes: yes. That host permits a cross-origin read. The app COULD fetch and store pictures from it, if that trade is made deliberately.
  • Displays: yes — Bytes: no. The usual answer. The picture can be shown by pointing at their server, which is hotlinking: it tells them every time your board is drawn, breaks when their CDN moves, and shows nothing offline. It cannot be copied in, so there is nothing to store.
  • Displays: no. Wrong address, a host that refuses hotlinking outright, or not an image. Check the address before concluding anything about CORS.
  • Refused by this page's own policy. Named separately wherever it happens, because it is the one failure that is about this app rather than about them.

What a yes would cost, before anyone builds it

  • "Nothing is fetched, nothing is sent" stops being true. That sentence is in the app's own About screen and it is the thing the app is. Narrowing it is a product decision, not a technical one.
  • That host learns your address, and when. Every catalogued model becomes a request from your machine to their server, which is a record of what you are printing and when you looked.
  • It would be off unless switched on, said plainly at the switch, with a way to remove pictures already fetched. None of that is built.
  • It buys the picture and nothing else. The name, the designer and the description live on the PAGE, which a browser may not read cross-origin at all. A link would still fill the title from the address, exactly as it does now.

A diagnostic. Not linked from the app, not indexed, and not cached by it. Its results are about the sites you type in, on the machine you type them on.
